#4471 is marked known: one bug, one fix queued. It is not one bug. Its reports were merged on the subject line (decision 0001), and a subject line says only that a date is wrong. Read by tenant, form and what the body shows, the ticket holds three problems. Each now has a ticket of its own.

The queued parser fix closes only the first row below. The other two stay open after it ships.

The three problems

parser: the shared parser reads every date month first

Every tenant in #4471 writes dates day first. The shared parser reads them month first, so day and month swap: 7 April becomes 4 July (R-41200). It hits every tenant, through the app, email and the booking-v2 form. This is the fix that is queued.

hint: booking-v3 tells people to type month first

The booking-v3 form prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field, then reads the date the tenant’s way, day first. People type what the hint says, so the date swaps before the parser sees it (R-41241, R-41305, R-41387). Only reports through the form of a booking-v3 tenant (Alder Physio, Copper Studio, Pale Harbour). The parser fix does not touch it: change the hint, or read the form month first.

one-day: the date moves by one day, not by a swap

These reports show no swap. The date lands one day early or late: 12 May shows as 11 May (R-41202), only June bookings are off by a day (R-41209), the invite comes before the booking (R-41236), Lisbon gets Tuesday and Toronto gets Monday for the same meeting (R-41344), Tuesday 6 October shows as Monday (R-41620). That points at time zones or summer time, not at date order. The desk read them this way (desk-notes.json); the old table filed them under parser by tenant.

Lessons

  • A subject line tells you a date is wrong, not why. Split by cause before you count.
  • Count report ids, not rows.
  • An intake tag is a guess. Check it against the tenant before you trust it.
